AI Decision Coach

Responsible AI Practice for Enterprise Teams

A scenario-based learning prototype that lets employees make, explain, revise, and reflect on responsible AI-use decisions.

The challenge

Responsible AI use requires judgment, not just prompt skill.

In a risk-sensitive enterprise, operational teams may understand basic prompting but still need to decide what data can be used, whether a tool is approved, how output should be verified, and when to involve a human reviewer or internal owner. This prototype turns that judgment into brief, observable practice.

What I built

One customer-complaint-summary scenario with pathway selection, rationale and confidence capture, safeguard choices, policy-mapped rubric feedback, revision, transfer practice, and browser-local event capture.

The experience

A five-step learning loop

Design choices

Three deliberate decisions

Decision before coaching

The learner commits before guidance appears, preserving judgment practice and creating a baseline for revision.

Diagnostic feedback

Privacy, authorization, reliability, fairness, and human accountability remain distinct so feedback is actionable.

Bounded automation

Deterministic rubric logic is mapped to fictional policy guidance; no LLM is treated as the authority for evaluation.

Technical approach

A transparent prototype architecture

  1. Learner interaction
  2. Scenario state + event capture
  3. Deterministic rubric check
  4. Fictional policy guidance mapping
  5. Feedback + revision loop
  6. Browser-local event data
Implemented stack: HTML, CSS, Bootstrap 5, vanilla JavaScript, deterministic rubric logic, and browser-local localStorage.

The coach is a learning aid, not an operational decision-maker. All scenario and policy content is fictional; the prototype does not use customer, employee, health, or proprietary data.

For reviewers

Technical and research details

Prototype event model

The browser-local event model records scenario_started, decision_submitted, safeguards_submitted, feedback_viewed, decision_revised, and transfer_completed.

Event Captured data
decision_submitted Pathway, selected risk factors, confidence
feedback_viewed Risk dimensions flagged by the rubric
decision_revised Original/revised pathway and confidence
transfer_completed Transfer response and calculated time on task

This is a prototype event model, not enterprise telemetry, xAPI, LMS, or LRS implementation.

Fictional policy guidance and safeguards

The Northstar Enterprise AI Use Standard supplies a transparent fictional policy basis for feedback:

  • Data Classification and Approved Tools
  • Human Review and Accountability
  • Reliability and Source Verification
  • Fairness, Bias, and Escalation
  • Accessibility and Inclusive Communication

The prototype does not provide operational, legal, security, privacy, HR, or compliance decisions.

Research and validation plan

Before production development, I would conduct 5–7 discovery interviews with representative operational staff, privacy/security partners, and managers. Findings would inform scenario realism, vocabulary, available decision paths, and escalation ownership.

A subsequent evaluation would examine decision reasoning, risk-signal recognition, confidence calibration, usability, and keyboard/screen-reader-informed accessibility.

I designed and built a scenario-based AI learning prototype that combines learning strategy, visible UX decisions, bounded rubric-based coaching, human-review safeguards, and a prototype measurement model.
