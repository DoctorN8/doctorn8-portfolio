AI Video Storyboards & Production Workflows

Multi-Model AI Generation, Synthetic Audio, and Editorial Workflows

The projects on this page explore how prompt-ready storyboards, model selection, synthetic audio, and light editorial work can produce coherent short-form videos for brand storytelling and training use cases. This is to demonstrate my workflow and creative process, not just the finished clips.

Overview of the Projects

Project: Ember & Ash Coffee Brand Ad

Project Context

The goal was to prototype a 30 second coffee brand spot built entirely from AI video clips, while still feeling like a carefully crafted cinematic ad. The storyboard emphasized a single rustic café environment, macro pour and steam shots, human touch, and a clean logo/tagline card rather than heavy text.

What I Designed

I wrote a six-shot storyboard specifying duration, camera language, lighting, and VO options for each frame. The shots move from wide café context into macro pour and steam hero shots, then out again to a hand on the cup and a broader environment, ending on a static brand card. Each prompt carried a locked environment description so models would keep the counter, light, and palette consistent across generations.

Tools Used

  • AI video models: Wan 2.6 / Wan 2.7 and Hailuo 2.3 / 2.3 Fast for short 6–10s clips.
  • Music: Suno-generated acoustic / lo-fi bed aligned to the ad’s pacing.
  • Voiceover: Optional single-line VO via ElevenLabs (“Some mornings deserve more than instant.”).
  • Editing: CapCut for clip selection, dissolves, timing, and logo/tagline card integration.

The final clip combines generated café shots, synthetic music, and optional VO into a 37 second spot that can stand alone or sit inside a broader brand demonstration.

Dust motes in the light, soft bokeh shelves and pendant lights in the background, shallow depth of field on the counter. Gentle lateral camera drift to introduce the atmosphere. In practice, each storyboard row above was treated as a discrete prompt for Wan or Hailuo, with 2–3 variations generated and the best clip selected before timing and transitions were locked in CapCut.

Project: Aviation Ground-Crew Safety Briefing

Project Context

This project explored whether a short training video, delivered by a synthetic safety officer, could maintain character consistency and instructional clarity across multiple PPE callouts and a closing reminder. The storyboard targeted a 30–45 second briefing suitable for ground-crew onboarding or shift reminders.

What I Designed

I built a six-shot storyboard that keeps a single safety officer present from the establishing tarmac shot through PPE demonstrations and hazard awareness, then back to a closing call to action in the hangar. Each prompt reused a detailed character and costume description to preserve identity, while lower-third text and VO scripts were written to reinforce hearing protection, high-visibility gear, steel-toe boots, and safety zones.

Tools Used

  • AI video models: Wan 2.6 / 2.7 and Hailuo 2.3 variants for short presenter and environment clips.
  • Voiceover: ElevenLabs-generated script read as a neutral corporate briefing.
  • Music: Light, unobtrusive underscore to avoid competing with the VO.
  • Editing: CapCut for sequencing, PPE step labels, lower-third text, and final export.

The final piece presents a consistent safety officer introducing PPE steps and safety zones, with VO and text overlays timed to each visual callout.

Storyboard Excerpt

The excerpt below shows how the aviation storyboard combined character, environment, and instructional text for each shot. As with the coffee project, each row became a prompt for individual clip generation before assembly.

# Duration Shot Type Visual Intent / Prompt Summary VO / On-Screen Text
1 4s Wide establishing Airport tarmac at dusk with a generic aircraft silhouette, open hangar doors, and a single safety officer in high-visibility vest and hard hat standing in the mid-ground. Cool sky and warm hangar lights establish tone without any branded livery. VO introduces the briefing and invites the viewer to focus on self-protection. Lower-third label: “Ground-Crew Safety Briefing”.
2 4s Presenter intro Medium shot of the same safety officer in the hangar, clearly identifiable by face, vest, hard hat, and boots. Camera is stable with slight natural movement, emphasizing direct address to camera. VO line: “Before every shift, protect yourself and your crew: hearing protection, high-vis gear, and steel-toe boots are non-negotiable.” No baked text; space reserved for overlays.
3 5s PPE item 1 – hearing Close-up of the same officer putting on over-ear hearing protection headset, framed from chest up with hangar equipment blurred in the background. Motion is focused on the headset and hands to keep the action clear. VO explains when hearing protection is required. Lower-third text: “Step 1: Hearing Protection”.
4 5s PPE item 2 – high-vis & boots Medium tarmac shot at dusk where the same officer gestures to their high-vis vest and steel-toe boots. Aircraft silhouette remains generic; hangar lighting provides rim light on the officer. VO reinforces visibility and foot protection. Lower-thirds: “Step 2: High-Visibility Gear” and “Steel-Toe Boots”.
6 4s Closing / call to action Medium shot back in the hangar, with the officer nodding confidently toward camera. Extra negative space at the bottom of frame leaves room for a closing text overlay. VO repeats the core message. On-screen text: “Safety First. Every Shift.”
Character consistency across these shots depended on a single, detailed description of the safety officer and environment, reused verbatim in every prompt and supported by reference frames during generation. CapCut then handled VO alignment and lower-third timing.

Project: Cognitive Load Theory Microlearning

Project Context

This project explores how AI avatar generation can accelerate instructional video production without sacrificing andragogical rigor. The goal was to create a focused, 1-minute microlearning asset that introduces career-switcher instructional designers to the three types of cognitive load—intrinsic, extraneous, and germane—using clear, relatable language.

What I Designed

Instead of starting from a manual storyboard, I used Synthesia’s AI-powered Custom Template workflow to generate a structured first draft from a targeted prompt. I then applied targeted instructional edits to the raw script: softening academic phrasing, adding visual and concrete examples (such as cluttered slide layouts vs. medical terminology), and selecting an avatar, voice, and visual layout that reflected a supportive, accessible tone for beginners.

Tools Used

  • AI video & avatar platform: Synthesia (Custom Template prompt-to-script workflow, avatar, and synthetic VO).
  • Scripting & ID edits: Manual script refinement for audio pacing, plain language, and concrete examples.
  • Visual & accessible design: High-contrast layout design and 60-second pacing optimization.

The final 60-second microlearning clip pairs an AI presenter with clear text overlays and refinement-focused script edits designed specifically for beginner instructional designers.

Draft Refinement Log

Because this project originated from a prompt rather than a traditional storyboard, the table below outlines the specific instructional edits applied to transform the raw AI output into a learner-centered draft.

Focus Area Raw AI Output / Initial Intent Instructional Design Edit Rationale
Opening Hook Formal academic definition of cognitive load theory. Smoothed the transition from the hook into a conversational lead-in. Lowers initial barrier to entry for career switchers unfamiliar with academic jargon.
Intrinsic Load Abstract description of mental effort required for tasks. Added a concrete comparison (medical terminology vs. everyday vocabulary). Gives learners an immediate, tangible anchor for task complexity.
Extraneous Load Generalized statement about poor instructional materials. Made language visual and specific (cluttered slides, confusing layouts). Focuses on directly actionable design mistakes designers can control.
Germane Load Technical definition of schema acquisition and processing. Reframed as productive, positive effort that designers actively facilitate. Shifts perspective from passive absorption to active schema construction.
Closing CTA Generic summary ending statement. Tied message directly back to the ID's core role: making learning easier. Reinforces professional identity and provides a clean takeaway for onboarding.
Rather than accepting the AI output as final footage, this workflow treats the tool as a rapid prototyping engine. The instructional value comes directly from evaluating, pruning, and shaping the draft to fit the target audience's exact needs.

Workflow and Production Pattern

How These Videos Were Built

Across all three projects, the workflow followed a repeatable pattern:

  • 01. Design intent first. Start with a written shot table or targeted AI prompt specifying audience, duration, visual intent, and core instructional goals.
  • 02. Model selection & draft generation. Select Wan, Hailuo, or Synthesia depending on whether the asset requires atmosphere, presenter consistency, or structured slide-and-avatar layouts.
  • 03. Audio & VO integration. Align synthetic narration (ElevenLabs/Synthesia) and background music beds (Suno) to specific beats rather than relying on generic narration.
  • 04. Editorial refinement. Apply critical design edits to raw script drafts and video clips, assembling final assets with transitions, overlays, and branding in CapCut or Synthesia.

What This Demonstrates

These examples show how to blend storyboard discipline, AI prompting, model selection, and instructional judgment. The coffee spot demonstrates cinematic atmosphere; the aviation briefing shows presenter consistency across procedural steps; and the cognitive load project highlights how rapid AI drafts are elevated through targeted edits.
